# Critique: current app and spec (2026-11-21)

This critique has three parts: a design review of the running app, a simulated expert-PM pitch review, and user personas built from real App Store reviews. It ends with proposed spec changes.

## 1. Design critique of the current app (impeccable)
Method: two independent reviewers. One was a design-director review on a 375px phone viewport. The other was a deterministic detector scan (CLI plus an in-browser overlay).

**Score: 25 out of 40 (Acceptable).** Cognitive load: 5 of 8 checks fail.

| Heuristic | Score | Key issue |
|---|---|---|
| Visibility of status | 3 | Breathing phase changes are not announced to screen readers |
| Match the real world | 2 | "Pace gate", "Raid", "Gates" and "XP" are system words, not the user's |
| User control | 2 | Exit ends a session instantly, with no confirmation and no partial credit |
| Consistency | 3 | The energy picker and run buttons are duplicated across tabs |
| Error prevention | 3 | The backup is an editable raw JSON box |
| Recognition over recall | 2 | The pre-call ritual is about 3.6 screens down the Status tab |
| Flexibility | 2 | No fast path for "I have a call soon" |
| Minimalist design | 2 | Status has 9 sections and is 3,126px tall; Guide is 3,762px |
| Error recovery | 3 | Error messages are clear |
| Help | 3 | The Guide is thorough but buried under game words |

**Verdict on specificity.** The design is clearly made for this app, not generic: the ruled status sheet, hexagonal rank, mosaic and breath marks are its own. But it was designed for the old product, a combat and grading heads-up display for a single user. Calm, Headspace and Breathwrk sell relief. This sells being tested.

**Priority issues**
- **P0.** The most valuable moment, a call 2 minutes away, is the hardest to reach. The pre-call ritual sits at about y=2,910px.
- **P1.** Home is a scoreboard. The first things you see are a grade E, "0 / 150" and "0 d · best 0", a ledger of what you lack.
- **P1.** The vocabulary and tone are wrong for an anxious adult: uppercase condensed type, "Locked", "Boss", "Raid".
- **P2.** Exit is unforgiving and sits top-right, out of thumb reach.
- **P2.** Accessibility:
  - "Open" buttons have no context in their accessible names.
  - There is no aria-live region on breathing phases.
  - The mosaic numbers have 3.3:1 contrast.
  - The type scale uses 7 sizes between 12 and 22px.

**Detector results.**
- Real findings: the mosaic contrast token and a 3px side stripe on the "twist" callout.
- False positives: 7 tiny-text hits on screen-reader-only spans.

**Strengths to keep**
- Honest copy: "Not today is a fine answer", "Rate your fluency, not your effort".
- The paced reader, with words lighting up, breath marks and timed pauses.
- Focus handling and the inert background behind drills.

## 2. Pitch review: how expert PMs would react
Imagine pitching the spec to a growth PM from Duolingo, a content PM from Calm, a clinical speech-language pathologist (SLP), and an App Store reviewer.

**What lands**
- **An underserved category with weak incumbents.** Stamurai is the leader with only 813 US ratings (4.67). The 2026 field is full of new single-developer AI apps with no ratings at all. A credible, beautifully made, honest app can stand out.
- **Competitor weak spots are known and fixable.** Real reviews complain about price ("$20 a week", "$30 a month"), paywall and login bugs, content that is "kid flash cards" or "insults your intelligence", "no way to know if you're doing it right", and practice words that don't match the user's feared sounds.
- **Honest, private, no account.** This is a real differentiator against StopStutter's "cure" messaging.

**What gets challenged**
1. **"Where is the aha in the first 5 minutes?"** A weekly re-record means the first visible proof arrives on day 7, and most users are gone by then. You need same-session proof.
2. **"How do you retain without notifications?"** The PWA can't remind reliably on iOS. The PWA is a beta channel, not the launch; native needs to come earlier.
3. **"How will you learn?"** There are zero analytics, so you can't see activation or retention. You need opt-in, privacy-first counting before launch.
4. **"Is Solo Leveling a moat or a filter?"** To an anime fan, Rank E reads as "a hunter at the start". To a 45-year-old manager, it reads as a failing grade. Keep the leveling, but make it a reward layer, not the first thing on the screen.
5. **"Four contexts on day one is a lot of content to make well."** Phone calls are among the most-feared situations for people who stammer. Launch with phone calls and work, and add interviews and social conversation after.
6. **"Who distributes it?"** SLPs and stammering communities. The therapist summary is a channel: make it shareable, and get an SLP to review the content before launch.
7. **"What does progress mean?"** XP measures taps. The number that matters is brave moments: real conversations you approached. Make that the headline number in Progress.

## 3. Users (from real reviews and the personas)
- **Priya, 31.** Opens the app 2 minutes before a dreaded call. She needs one button and her opening line. Today she gets a grade and a 10-minute run.
- **Marcus, 24, anime fan.** Loves the System voice and ranks, and wants titles and the feeling of levelling up.
- **Helen, 46, manager.** Would delete the app if it felt like a game for teenagers. Wants calm, an adult tone, and proof it works.
- **Dana, SLP.** Would recommend the app if technique practice is correct, it never promises a cure, and the summary is useful in a session.

All four want different surfaces of the same app. The resolution is two layers: a calm surface everyone sees, and a leveling layer that shows up at reward moments and in Progress.

## 4. Proposed spec changes
1. **Same-session proof as the first-run aha.** Record a 20-second cold read, do 2 minutes of breathe-and-pace, re-read, then hear both. Keep the weekly re-record as well.
2. **"Speaking soon" is the hero.** It is the first and largest element on Home. The daily session comes second.
3. **Calm surface, leveling layer.** Plain names:
   - Breathe, Read aloud, Word warm-up, Practice questions, Real-world steps.
   - Mixed-case type, an adult tone, nothing shown as "Locked".
   - Rank, the System voice and titles appear at reward moments and in Progress, never as the first thing on Home.
   - A visual redesign from a combat heads-up display to a calm instrument.
4. **Feared words and sounds.** The user enters their own name, feared sounds and words they avoid, and drills weave them in.
5. **"Am I doing it right?" checks.** After each recording, show a short listen-for checklist: soft start, steady pace, pause at the marks. Short written technique demos. SLP-recorded audio examples come later.
6. **Forgiving session control.** One End control within thumb reach, with confirmation, and partial time still counts.
7. **Brave moments as the north-star number** in Progress, alongside rank.
8. **Launch with 2 contexts** (phone calls and work). Interviews and social conversation come in a later update.
9. **Native sooner.** The Capacitor build and TestFlight come right after recording, because reminders and haptics need it. The PWA stays as the beta channel.
10. **Opt-in, privacy-first analytics** before the App Store launch. Choosing the service needs your approval.
11. **Accessibility fixes:**
    - aria-live regions for breathing phases.
    - Contextual names on buttons.
    - A 5-size type scale.
    - 4.5:1 contrast.
12. **SLP channel.** A shareable therapist summary, and an SLP review of the content before launch.
